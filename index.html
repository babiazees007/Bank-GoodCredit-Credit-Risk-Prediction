Project Introduction

What we are solving and why it matters

What is credit risk?

Credit risk is the possibility that a borrower will fail to repay a loan or credit card bill on time. Every missed payment is a potential loss for the bank.

Why predict it?

  • Reduce losses by acting early (payment plans, limit changes)
  • Approve new lending more carefully
  • Offer better rates to low-risk customers
  • Meet regulatory risk-management requirements

Target variable: Bad_label

ValueMeaning
0Good credit – pays on time
1Bad credit – 30+ days past due (30 DPD+)
Gini coefficient: measures how well the model separates good from bad customers.
Gini = (2 × ROC-AUC − 1) × 100
0 = random guessing, 100 = perfect. Business-case benchmark: 37.9.

Data Cleaning

Fix the data before trusting any result
  • Dates converted from text to real dates (day first)
  • Payment-history strings cleaned of extra quote characters
  • Numeric columns converted; non-numbers treated as missing
  • Duplicates checked; one row per customer enforced in demographics
  • High-missingness features (over 95% empty) flagged for removal
Why not fill or delete everything? A missing closed_dt means the account is still open, and a missing amt_past_due usually means nothing is overdue. Missing values are interpreted, not blindly filled.

  • Feature distributions: several features differ between good and bad customers
  • Correlation: no single feature is strongly correlated with the target, which is typical in credit risk

Prepare Data for Machine Learning

Split, impute, scale, and protect against leakage

  • Median imputation: missing values filled with the training median
  • Scaling: applied for Logistic Regression only
  • Leakage control: preprocessing lives inside a Pipeline, learned from training folds only

Final Business Conclusion

Summary for a non-technical audience

Business problem: rank credit card customers by how likely they are to fall 30+ days behind, so the bank can act early.

Recommendations

Prioritise early support and monitoring for the highest-risk deciles.
Use the model as one input to credit decisions, with human review.
Agree the probability cut-off with the business, based on the real cost of missed risky customers versus wrongly flagged good ones.
Confirm feature definitions and the benchmark population before presenting the Gini comparison as final.

Future improvements

Time-based validation Build skipped features when data allows Probability calibration Per-customer SHAP explanations Ongoing monitoring and retraining
